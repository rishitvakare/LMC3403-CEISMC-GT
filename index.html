01 / The opportunity gap

Access to school
is only the
beginning.

Attending school does not guarantee equal opportunities to explore STEM. Students across different school districts face systemic gaps in access to advanced coursework, hands-on learning materials, and academic enrichment.

These disparities directly shape student confidence, career aspirations, and future possibilities. Bridging this divide requires deliberate partnerships between university networks and local K–12 classrooms.

Racial & Socioeconomic Divide in STEM

Unequal access to advanced coursework.

National U.S. Department of Education data from 2020–2021 reveals that calculus was offered at only 35% of high schools with high Black and Latino enrollment, compared to 54% of schools with low Black and Latino enrollment—a 19 percentage point gap in opportunity.

Source: U.S. Department of Education CRDC ↗

Long-Term Impact of K-12 STEM Exposure

Early experiences shape future careers.

Research demonstrates that early exposure yields lasting results: taking a high school computer science course increases the likelihood of a student going on to earn a Computer Science bachelor’s degree by approximately 5 percentage points.

03 / Your classroom, connected

A bridge between
Georgia Tech
and your school.

The Center for Education Integrating Science, Mathematics, and Computing (CEISMC), part of Georgia Tech’s College of Lifetime Learning, connects university innovation directly with K–12 schools.

Through hands-on learning, teacher development, strategic partnerships, and research, CEISMC expands access to STEM/STEAM education for every student.

05 / Learning by doing

Real projects.
Real possibilities.

In addition to supporting teacher development and helping with hybrid instruction for students, CEISMC believes in the power of hands-on learning. Through hands-on projects, students apply computer science to real-world challenges, explore emerging technologies, and discover how they can use their skills to make a meaningful impact in their communities.
