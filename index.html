01 / The opportunity gap

Access to school
is only the beginning.

Unequal access

Every student deserves the opportunity to explore their interests, develop new skills, and imagine a future in STEM. But access to a classroom does not always mean access to the same educational opportunities.

Students attend schools with different levels of access to advanced coursework, hands-on activities, academic support, and enrichment programs. These differences can shape their confidence, interests, and future pathways.

Long-term impacts

Early experiences with computer science can influence whether students see it as a subject they can pursue. Research from Maryland found that taking a high school computer science course increased the likelihood of earning a computer science bachelor's degree by approximately 5 percentage points.

03 / Your classroom, connected

A bridge between
Georgia Tech
and your school.

Georgia Tech's Center for Education Integrating Science, Mathematics, and Computing (CEISMC) connects university expertise with K–12 communities across Georgia. Through partnerships with schools and educators, CEISMC creates opportunities for students to explore science, technology, engineering, and mathematics through meaningful, hands-on learning.

One example is the Rural Computer Science Initiative, which works with rural Georgia school communities to expand access to computer science education. Through teacher professional development, classroom resources, and engaging student experiences, the initiative helps bring new opportunities to schools that may otherwise have limited access to these resources.

Discover CEISMC ↗
01

For students

Discover new interests through hands-on STEM activities, creative problem-solving, and real-world projects. These experiences help students build confidence, develop practical skills, and see how computer science connects to the world around them.

02

For educators

Strengthen classroom instruction with professional development, research-informed teaching strategies, and access to new resources. Educators can explore engaging ways to introduce computer science and bring emerging STEM topics into their classrooms.

03

For schools

Build lasting connections with Georgia Tech through outreach programs, curriculum support, and community-based partnerships. These collaborations help schools create more opportunities for students to engage with STEM both inside and beyond the classroom.

04 / From the gap to the connection

Expanding STEM opportunities across Georgia.

CEISMC works with schools and communities throughout Georgia to make STEM learning more accessible. Through initiatives like the Rural Computer Science Initiative, these partnerships help bring educational resources, teacher support, and hands-on experiences to students across the state. By connecting Georgia Tech's expertise with local needs, CEISMC helps create opportunities for more students to explore technology and envision future pathways in computer science.

HOW CAN I GET STARTED AS A STUDENT?

Beyond the classroom.
Where could you start?

Not every student has access to these opportunities through their school. But that doesn't mean you have to wait to get started. Whether you're discovering computer science for the first time or looking for your next challenge, there's a way to explore.

You don't need experience, expensive equipment, or even an idea to get started. Tell us a little about what interests you, and we'll help you find a starter project that's right for you. And if you discover a passion for CS along the way, CEISMC's programs and educational opportunities can help you take that interest even further.
